# Phishing and Security Incident Reporting

**Category:** Security  
**Last updated:** January 2024  
**Owner:** IT Security

## Recognising a Phishing Attempt

Phishing emails try to trick you into clicking a malicious link, downloading malware, or handing over credentials. Common warning signs:

- Sender address does not match the claimed organisation (e.g., support@c0mpany.com)
- Unexpected urgency ("Your account will be deleted in 24 hours")
- Generic greetings ("Dear user") rather than your name
- Links that show a different URL when hovered over
- Attachments you were not expecting, especially .zip, .exe, or macro-enabled Office files
- Requests for passwords, MFA codes, or financial transfers — legitimate internal teams will never ask for these by email

## What to Do If You Receive a Suspicious Email

**Do not** click any links, download attachments, or reply to the sender.

1. Click the **Report Phishing** button in Gmail (the shield icon in the email toolbar)
2. Forward the email as an attachment to **security@company.com**
3. Delete the email from your inbox

Reporting takes less than 30 seconds and helps IT block the threat for everyone.

## What to Do If You Clicked a Link or Entered Credentials

Act immediately — speed matters:

1. **Do not** close the browser or shut down the computer
2. Disconnect from Wi-Fi (do not use the VPN disconnect — physically turn off Wi-Fi or unplug ethernet)
3. Call the IT Security line immediately: ext. 4358 (available 24/7 for incidents)
4. Do not attempt to fix it yourself — preserving evidence is critical

IT Security will guide you through the next steps, which may include a password reset, MFA re-enrolment, and device scan.

## Other Security Incidents to Report

Report any of the following immediately to security@company.com or ext. 4358:

- Lost or stolen device (see also: Hardware Request Policy)
- Suspected unauthorised access to your account (unexpected sign-in alerts, unknown sent emails)
- Accidental sharing of confidential data to the wrong person or system
- Ransomware or unusual file encryption on your machine
- Suspicious USB devices found in the office

## Reporting Timeline

| Incident type | Report within |
|---------------|--------------|
| Phishing click / credential entry | Immediately (call ext. 4358) |
| Lost/stolen device | 1 hour |
| Accidental data disclosure | 2 hours |
| Suspicious email (no click) | Same business day |

Under GDPR and applicable data protection laws, the company must report certain data breaches to the regulator within 72 hours of becoming aware. Employee delays in reporting are the most common reason this deadline is missed. **Report first, investigate later.**

## No-Blame Culture

IT Security operates a no-blame reporting culture. The goal is rapid containment, not punishment. Employees who report incidents promptly — even if they caused them — are treated with discretion. What is not acceptable is knowingly delaying or concealing an incident.
